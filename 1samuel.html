1 Samuel moves from the last of the judges to the first two kings, and asks what kind of leader a people after God's own heart really needs.

What happens in 1 Samuel

The book opens with Hannah, a barren woman whose desperate prayer is answered with a son, Samuel, whom she dedicates to the Lord's service from childhood. Samuel grows to become Israel's last judge and a prophet, but as he ages, the people demand a king like the surrounding nations, and God gives them Saul — tall, impressive, and ultimately unwilling to fully obey. When Saul's disobedience costs him the kingdom, Samuel is sent, almost in secret, to anoint a shepherd boy named David, the least likely of his brothers, because the Lord does not see as man sees. David rises to public notice by facing the Philistine giant Goliath with nothing but a sling and complete confidence in the name of the Lord, and from there spends years as a fugitive, hunted by an increasingly jealous and unstable Saul, even while repeatedly refusing the chance to kill the king who wants him dead. The book ends with Saul's tragic death in battle, his reign a cautionary tale about kingship without a surrendered heart.

Why it matters

1 Samuel teaches that God's choices rarely match human expectations — He passes over the obvious candidate and chooses the shepherd, He values a surrendered heart over an impressive appearance, and He protects His anointed even in the wilderness years before the throne ever comes. It sets up the entire story of David, the king through whose line the promised Messiah would one day come.
